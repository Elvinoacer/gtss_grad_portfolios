Everything marked **COPY** is ready to paste into WhatsApp, LinkedIn, Instagram, X, university groups, or direct messages. Pick the message for the audience; don't post all of them together. Treat any sample profiles, names, projects, and accomplishments as demonstrations unless their identities and claims have been independently verified with their owners.

# 12. Common objections: ready-to-send replies

**“I don't have experience.”**  
You don't need to invent work experience. If you've completed class projects, lab work, fieldwork, teaching practice, research, attachment or independent work, we can identify what honestly demonstrates your thinking and growth. The portfolio is built around real evidence, not inflated claims.

**“Isn't my CV enough?”**  
You still need your CV. The website complements it. A CV gives a concise overview; your portfolio makes it easier to explore the actual projects and work behind the highlights.

**“I'm not in tech.”**  
That's exactly why our designs vary by profession. Engineers may lead with drawings, teachers with teaching practice, journalists with published work, and researchers with methods and findings. You shouldn't have to look like a developer to have a good portfolio.

**“Will it guarantee a job or scholarship?”**  
No honest provider can guarantee that. A portfolio can help you present clearer evidence and make your application easier to examine, but selection still depends on requirements, competition, experience and many other factors.

**“Will I appear at the top of Google or in ChatGPT answers?”**  
We can build a public website using search-friendly structure and relevant content, which can improve discoverability over time. Search indexing, rankings, and appearances in AI-generated answers are controlled by those platforms and cannot be guaranteed.

**“Can I add new experience later?”**  
That's the idea of a living professional portfolio: it can evolve as your work evolves. The exact future update or maintenance arrangement should be confirmed with GTSS before purchase.

**“What if my course isn't in the gallery?”**  
You can still ask us to assess it. Our current gallery is the starting point, not the limit. We'd identify the types of evidence that matter in your field before planning the design.

# 15. Suggested campaign rollout

### Week 1 — The problem students recognise

Publish **“Two pages can't tell a four-year story”** with an actual CV-to-portfolio visual transformation. Run the six-frame story. Ask viewers which project they wish more people could see.

### Week 2 — Demonstrate the product

Post individual screen recordings of three existing demos, each explaining why its design differs by discipline. Show specific project sections, not just the home screen. Avoid claiming the demo is a verified person's real work unless it is.

### Week 3 — Enter new academic disciplines

Share the education, arts, healthcare and agriculture messages as **invitations to shape the next portfolio**, not claims that completed templates already exist. Ask student groups which artefacts and achievements a credible portfolio should include.

### Week 4 — Convert interest into consultation

Share the practical “What goes into mine?” message and the objection answers. Invite students to submit their discipline and a list of real work. Offer a thoughtful assessment before quoting the scope or promising a publication date.

**Measure:** link clicks, meaningful replies, portfolio consultation requests, number of qualified project inventories received, and paid conversions when a commercial offer is in place. Do not use fabricated testimonials or unsupported employment outcomes.

# 16. Truthful marketing and operational safeguards

1. **Search visibility:** You can optimise technical SEO, page content and indexing eligibility. You cannot guarantee a first-page Google result or that ChatGPT will mention an individual. Google explicitly does not guarantee crawling, indexing or ranking.
2. **Sample data:** Some earlier sample ideas used fictional names or placeholder work. Label demonstrations appropriately and obtain permission before describing any portfolio as a verified individual's achievements or featuring their likeness.
3. **Academic trust:** Never present the collection as an officially endorsed Moi University programme unless such endorsement actually exists.
4. **Sensitive materials:** Don't publish patient information, identifiable schoolchildren, confidential employer code, restricted attachment reports, private research data, or copyrighted material without authorisation.
5. **Recorded intro videos:** Offer this as an optional feature only once production, editing, consent and hosting are actually supported. Explain that it complements, rather than replaces, interviews.
6. **Domains and maintenance:** State what is included and for how long before accepting payment — custom domain, hosting, updates, edits, ownership and handover.
7. **Evidence-led copy:** Use a concrete real project breakdown or screenshot in campaigns whenever possible. Visual proof will outperform grand claims if people can see the product working.
8. **Outcome claims:** Avoid “guaranteed scholarships,” “get hired faster,” “rank #1,” or “AI will know you.” Those promises aren't supported by a website alone.
